BUFFALO keeps **one record per person**, owned by the LGA that recorded it.
Other jurisdictions cannot see it, but a search can tell them that a record
exists so that they can ask for access.

## Person records

Open **Intelligence → Persons**. A person record holds names and other names
(aliases), date of birth or approximate age, gender, nationality, occupation,
a description, photos, roles (for example witness, suspect, traditional
ruler), and associations with other persons.

- **NIN, phone number, address and date of birth are encrypted.** They are
  shown only to those allowed to see the record, and you can still search by
  NIN or phone number.
- Before saving a new person, BUFFALO checks for likely duplicates (names,
  aliases, NIN, phone, date of birth). Use the existing record if it is the
  same person. If you save anyway you must explain why; this is recorded.
- Photos are scanned for malware before they are accepted.

## Persons of interest

A person becomes a **person of interest** from their record (**Designate as
person of interest**). The person of interest record adds the reason, risk level,
status, last sighting and a review date. You are reminded when a review is
due. Clearing a person of interest (no longer of interest) is done by the
State Director, DG or DDG.

## Searching for a person

Use the search on **Persons**:

- Search by **NIN** or **phone number** for an exact match.
- Search by **name**, and add the **date of birth** if you know it.

Results in your own jurisdiction appear as normal results. If a matching
record exists **in another state**, you may instead see a notice:

| Notice | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Record found** | Same NIN or phone number |
| **Likely match** | Very similar name **and** the same date of birth |

A name on its own never produces a notice. The notice shows only the state
and whether it is a person of interest: no name, no details. You may not see
any notice if the record is above your clearance.

The number of notices per officer per day is limited; the national level is
told about unusual volumes.

## Requesting access (LGA Security Chief, State Director, Deputy)

From the notice, choose **Request access** and fill in:

- **Reason** (at least 30 characters): why you need this record.
- **Reference** (optional): your incident or case reference.
- **Scope:** **profile** (identity, photo, aliases, roles) or **full**
  (also NIN, phone, address, person of interest details, offices, groups and
  associations).
- **Urgency:** normal or urgent.
- **Duration:** 7, 14 or 30 days.

The owning state's State Director (or the Deputy, if the Director has
delegated approvals) is notified. Unanswered requests are escalated to the
national level: urgent ones after 2 hours, normal ones after 48 hours.

## Deciding a request (State Director, delegated Deputy)

Open **Intelligence → Access requests**, tab **Awaiting my decision**. For each
request you see who asks, why and for how long. You can:

- **Approve**, possibly for a shorter time or only the profile (never more
  than requested). You are asked to confirm with your authenticator code.
- **Reject**, with a note.

You never decide your own request, and you must be cleared for the record.

### Delegating approvals (State Director)

On **Access requests**, tab **Delegations**, a State Director chooses
**Delegate** to let the Deputy State Director decide requests from a start
date, optionally until an end date.
The delegation can be revoked at any time.

## Using a grant

An approved request is a **grant**: read-only access to that person, for the
granted time and scope. The person then appears for you with a notice that
you are viewing under a grant. A grant:

- never lets you change the record;
- never shows the LGA, community, photo gallery, history or linked records;
- never goes above your clearance;
- ends at the expiry date (you are reminded 24 hours before) or when revoked,
  and an open page closes at once.

Every view under a grant is recorded. Your own requests and grants are on the
**My requests** and **Grants** tabs; the owning state sees who searched its
records on **Searches of our records**.
