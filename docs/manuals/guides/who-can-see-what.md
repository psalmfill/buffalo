title: Who can see what
summary: How jurisdiction and classification decide which records you see, and the only two ways records cross jurisdictions.
audience: all

---

BUFFALO decides what you see in two steps, every time. Hiding a button is
never the protection: the server checks both steps on every page, list,
search, map, dashboard, export and printed report.

1. **Your permissions** decide *what you can do*: view incidents, write a
   security report, review it, export a list.
2. **Your jurisdiction and the record's classification** decide *which
   records* you can see.

## Jurisdiction: where you work

Every record belongs to a place, an LGA and its state, and you see only the
records of your own place.

| Your role | You see records of |
|---|---|
| LGA Security Chief | **Your own LGA only**: never another LGA of your state, never another state. |
| State Director, Deputy State Director | **Your own state only**: all of its LGAs, never another state. |
| Deputy Director General | **All states and LGAs**, or only the geopolitical zones assigned to you. |
| Director General | **All states and LGAs** nationwide. |
| System administrator (super admin) | **No intelligence records at all**: only accounts, settings, backups and the audit log. |

A record outside your jurisdiction is simply not there for you: it does not
appear in lists, counts, maps, dashboards, analytics, search or exports, and
opening its address shows "not found".

Some reference information is shared because it is background, not
intelligence:

- the list of states, LGAs, senatorial districts and wards;
- your own **state's reference profile** for LGA Security Chiefs (its
  overview, profile, senatorial districts and the public office holders
  their access shows), read-only;
- the national directory of organizations (name and sector). Their sites
  and contacts belong to an LGA like any other record;
- the names of officers who acted on a record you can see (for example who
  reviewed it), and nothing else about them.

## Classification: how sensitive a record is

Within your jurisdiction, a classified record is shown only to the ranks
cleared for it. Classification narrows what you see; it never widens your
jurisdiction.

| Level | Who may see it (always within their jurisdiction) |
|---|---|
| Restricted (the default) | Every intelligence role. |
| Confidential | DG, DDG, State Director, Deputy State Director, and the person who created it. |
| Secret | DG, DDG, State Director, and the person who created it. |

You cannot give a record a level you could not see yourself.

## The only two ways a record crosses jurisdictions

Nothing else, including security reports, incidents and evidence, is ever
shared across jurisdictions.

1. **Person access grants.** When you search for a person recorded in
   another state, you may see only a short "possible match" notice, with no
   details. You can ask for access. The owning State Director (or the
   Deputy, when the State Director has delegated it) decides. An approved
   grant shows that one person, read-only, for a limited time, and can be
   revoked. A grant never overrides classification, and a record above your
   clearance never produces a notice at all.
2. **Shared threats and flashpoints.** A state can share a threat report or
   a flashpoint, read-only, with named neighbouring states or LGAs. It
   appears under **Shared threats & flashpoints**. Classification still
   applies, and shared items are never counted as the recipient's own.

## Everything is recorded

Views of persons, persons of interest, classified records, evidence and
security report attachments, and every download, export and print, are
logged with who, when and from where.
