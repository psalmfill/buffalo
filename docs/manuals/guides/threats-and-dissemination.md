A **threat report** is an early warning: something that may happen. It is
different from an incident, which has happened.

## Creating a threat report

Open **Threats & flashpoints → Threat reports → Record threat report**:

- **Threat type** and a description of the threat.
- **Credibility:** unconfirmed, possible, probable or confirmed.
- **Urgency:**
  - **routine:** included in the daily digest to the State Director and Deputy;
  - **priority:** they are notified at once;
  - **immediate:** they and the DG and DDGs are notified at once.
- **Source type** (informant, community, agency report, open source, officer
  observation, other). **Never record an informant's identity.**
- **Where:** the LGA, and optionally a point or an area.
- **When:** the expected time window, and the expiry date (at most 90 days).
- Links to flashpoints, criminal groups (suspected or named in the report)
  and incidents.

The report gets a reference such as `THR/LA/2026/00012`.

## Status

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Active | The threat stands |
| Mitigated | Action has reduced it |
| Materialised | It happened (link the incident) |
| Expired | Its expiry date passed (a warning comes 24 hours before) |
| Withdrawn | It was wrong or no longer relevant |

Every status change needs a note. When an incident is submitted, BUFFALO
records the active threats it may have realised, so reviewers can link them.
An expired report can be **reactivated** by the State Director, DG or DDG.

LGA Security Chiefs manage only the threat reports they created.

## Sharing across borders (dissemination)

Threat reports and flashpoints are normally visible only in their own
jurisdiction and nationally. When a threat or flashpoint concerns a
neighbouring state or LGA, share it with them:

1. Open the threat report or flashpoint and choose **Disseminate**
   (State Director, Deputy, DG, DDG).
2. Choose the target states or LGAs and give a reason (at least 20
   characters).
3. The dialog warns you if officers at a target cannot see the record because
   of its classification. Classification is never overridden.

Recipients (the State Director and Deputy of a target state, the LGA chiefs
of target LGAs) are notified and see the record **read-only**, without its
History, in **Shared threats & flashpoints**, where they **Acknowledge** it.
They are reminded if they do not (after 2 hours for immediate threats).
A recipient State Director can pass it on to LGAs of their own state.

Sharing ends when the threat expires (flashpoints: after 90 days, renewable),
or when it is revoked; onward sharing ends with it.

**LGA Security Chiefs** cannot disseminate; they use **Request
dissemination**, which goes to their State Director.

BUFFALO also checks daily for immediate or priority threats and high or
severe flashpoints whose area reaches another LGA or state that has not been
told, and alerts the owning State Director.
