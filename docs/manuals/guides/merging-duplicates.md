---
title: Merging duplicate persons
summary: Finding records of the same person, requesting a merge, reviewing and merging, and undoing a merge.
audience: intelligence
order: 35
---

Sometimes the same person is recorded twice: two LGAs record the same
suspect, or a name is spelt two ways. Each copy then holds part of the
picture. **Merging** combines the two records into one.

Merges happen only **within one state**. Records of the same person in two
states stay separate; use an access request instead.

## Finding duplicates

Open **Intelligence → Duplicate persons**. The list shows pairs of records
in **your own** jurisdiction that may be the same person, strongest first:

| Strength | Why |
|---|---|
| Certain | Same NIN |
| Strong | Same phone number, or the same date of birth and a similar name |
| Possible | Nearly the same name (or alias) in the same LGA |

Records you can see only through temporary access never appear here.
State Directors also see last night's count for their state.

If two records are **not** the same person (for example twins), a State
Director can choose **Not a duplicate** and give the reason. The pair is not
suggested again.

## Requesting a merge

LGA security chiefs and Deputy State Directors request merges; the State
Director decides.

- From the list: **Request merge** on a pair.
- From a person's page: **Request merge**, then pick the other record
  (only records in your own jurisdiction can be picked).

Say why they are the same person (at least 20 characters), and which record
you suggest keeping. The State Director is notified. Requests are listed
under **Merge requests** on the Duplicate persons page.

## Reviewing and merging

State Directors, the DG and DDGs open **Review and merge** from the list,
from a request, or from a person's page (**Merge with…**).

The page shows both records side by side. The record on the left is kept
(the **survivor**); the one on the right (the **source**) is merged into it.
Use **Swap** to keep the other one.

- **Fields:** where only one record has a value, it is kept. Where both
  differ, the row is highlighted: choose which value to keep. Names that are
  not kept become **aliases**, so searches by the old name still find the
  person.
- **Two different NINs** mean two different people: these records **cannot
  be merged**.
- **Different dates of birth**, or only one record marked **deceased**,
  need an explicit confirmation.
- **Owning LGA:** when the records belong to different LGAs, choose which
  LGA owns the result. The page warns that officers of the other LGA will
  then see "Person on record (not visible to you)" on their incidents, and
  how many incidents that affects.
- **Classification:** the result takes the higher of the two.
- **What moves:** aliases, photos (choose the current photo), roles,
  associations, political offices, group memberships, incidents (including
  closed ones), and community, religious and organization links. If both
  are persons of interest, the two records are combined: the higher risk
  level, all offence categories, both sets of offences (labelled with the
  merged record's reference) and the latest sighting; you choose the status.
- **What ends:** temporary access that other states hold on the source is
  **revoked** and their open access requests are **cancelled** ("record
  merged"). They are never transferred to the survivor.

**Merge records** asks for a code from your authenticator app. The source
is removed from searches; its address takes you to the survivor (if you may
see it). The officers of both LGAs and the State Director are notified.

## Merged records and undo

The survivor's **Profile** lists its **Merged records**, with a link to each
one's **History** (history stays with the original record).

A merge can be **undone for 30 days** by a State Director, the DG or a DDG,
with a reason and an authenticator code:

- Both records exist again, each with exactly the links it had before.
  Links added to the survivor since the merge stay with it.
- If the survivor's details were edited since the merge, the dialog lists
  them: tick a field to keep its current value.
- Revoked temporary access and cancelled access requests are **not**
  restored.
- A merge cannot be undone once the survivor has been merged into another
  record, or after another record was merged into it later (undo that one
  first), or if a link has since been moved elsewhere.

## Correcting a political office's holder

If a political office was recorded against the wrong person, a State
Director, the DG or a DDG can open the person's **Political offices** tab and
choose **Reassign holder** on that office. Pick the correct person (from your
own jurisdiction) and give the reason (at least 20 characters). The correct
person becomes a political leader; the other person loses that role unless
they hold another current office. Both persons' histories record the change.

This is only for mistakes. When an office **changes hands**, edit the office
to set its end date and record the office for the new holder.
